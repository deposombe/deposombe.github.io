The Overlay

The overlay is the list a hotkey shows. This page describes what is on it, every way of picking from it, and the modes that change how it behaves: Show All, watching, and opening a whole palette.

Last updated 21 November 2026

What the Overlay Shows

  • Names in alphabetical order, in as many columns as fit. The letters to type next are highlighted.
  • A forced alias, if a trigger has one, as a small tag before its name.
  • A palette shown as one entry, with a chevron after its name. Picking it opens that palette in place.
  • An ellipsis (…) after a trigger that asks for input before it opens.
  • A padlock tag on a trigger with locked keys, showing those keys.
  • A New or Changed tag on a trigger that an import or an AI assistant added or changed and that has not been opened since.

The row above the list shows Rapidfire’s mark, the path when a palette other than General is showing, and three controls on the right: the eye (watching), the Show All button, and the hand switcher (Left, Both, Right).

When a palette holds more than fits the largest size allowed in Settings > Overlay, the list scrolls, and a line at the bottom says how many more there are. Typing is how a long list is narrowed; scrolling is for looking.

Pick a Trigger

  1. Type the highlighted letters. After each key, the list narrows and the next letters are highlighted.
  2. When one trigger is left, it opens.
KeysWhat they do
DeleteTakes back the last key. With nothing typed inside a folder, goes back to the palette it was opened from.
EscapeCloses the overlay.
Arrow keysMove a highlight from row to row. Nothing is highlighted until an arrow key is pressed.
ReturnOpens the highlighted row.
TabOpens the most used trigger still in the list, or the folder that holds it.
SpaceA key like any other when a name needs it. It is shown as ␣.

A row can also be clicked. Every way of picking does the same thing.

Tab needs usage counts, which are kept on this Mac for 30 days (Settings > General, Count how often triggers are used). Where nothing in the list has been opened yet, Tab says so.

A key that matches nothing closes the overlay. To keep it open instead, set A key that matches nothing to Is Ignored in Settings > Keys.

Folders

A palette can be shown inside another in two ways, chosen in the editor under Show Inside.

All TriggersEvery trigger of the inner palette is listed, each after the palette’s prefix: with the prefix h, Lights reads “h Lights”.
One EntryThe inner palette is a single row, a folder. Picking it shows that palette in place, and Delete goes back.

Show All

Show All opens every folder in place, including folders inside folders, so the whole library is visible at once.

KeysWhat they do
⌘TSwitches Show All on. It stays on until ⌘T is pressed again or the overlay closes.
⌥ (held)Shows the folders opened for as long as Option is held. The key must be held alone for a moment; Option used to type a letter or to drag a row does not trigger it.

The Show All button, to the left of the hand switcher, does the same as ⌘T. It is shown when the list has a folder.

  • Rows inside an opened folder are indented under it. A folder too long for one column continues in the next, under its name repeated in grey.
  • The opened view moves to the middle of the screen and may grow up to the size chosen in Settings > Overlay > Show All, independent of the overlay’s everyday maximum size.
  • When even that size is not enough, the view scrolls. With Shrink text to fit on, the text is made smaller first, down to the smallest text size.
  • Rows inside opened folders are picked with the pointer. Keys keep working for the rows of the palette that is showing.

Watching

A watched overlay stays open when another app takes the keyboard, and is drawn again whenever the library changes. It is meant for seeing changes as they arrive: an AI assistant adding triggers, an import, or an edit made on another Mac.

KeysWhat they do
⌘KSwitches watching on or off.

The eye button in the overlay’s top row does the same, and is filled while watching is on. It moves once for each change shown, unless Reduce Motion is on.

What was typed starts over when a watched overlay is redrawn, because a change can move keys. Escape, the hotkey, or picking a trigger closes a watched overlay as usual.

  • Watching and Presenting: The overlay can be kept open, redrawn as the library changes, and unfolded to show everything at once. Together with a few links and settings, this makes it possible to follow an AI assistant at work, and to demonstrate or record Rapidfire without touching the keyboard.

Open a Whole Palette

Holding Control while picking a trigger opens every trigger in that trigger’s palette, and in the palettes shown inside it, at any depth. It is meant for groups that belong together: the dashboards checked every morning, the tools of one project.

  • The palette’s own triggers open first, in the order they are listed, then the palettes inside it in the order of the editor’s sidebar.
  • Triggers in General cannot be opened this way, since that would open the whole library.
  • A trigger that asks for input is left out.
  • Settings > Keys > Confirm before opening many decides when Rapidfire asks first: Never, Over 10 Triggers, or Always. The question appears inside the overlay; Return opens, Escape cancels.

Other Keys in the Overlay

KeysWhat they do
⌘1, ⌘2, ⌘3Switch to the left hand, both hands, or the right hand until the overlay closes.
⌘+, ⌘−, ⌘0Make the text larger or smaller, or reset it. The size is saved.
⌘EOpens the editor.
⌘,Opens Settings.
⌘WCloses the overlay.

Other Command shortcuts do nothing in the overlay. Rapidfire does not become the active app when the overlay opens, so a shortcut such as ⌘Q is never taken from, or sent to, the app in front.

Pointer Styles

The row under the pointer is shown in one of about forty styles, from a faint outline to a row that grows, glows or is repeated in large type above the overlay. They are meant to be tried in the overlay itself until one suits.

KeysWhat they do
⌘]The next pointer style.
⌘[The previous pointer style.
  • A line in the overlay names each style as it is reached and gives its place in the list.
  • The style last stopped on is kept, on this Mac.
  • Quiet, a soft fill with a thin outline, is the first style and the one a new installation uses.
  • The styles change only how the row under the pointer looks. Picking, keys and the highlight moved with the arrow keys work the same in all of them.

Many styles move. With Reduce Motion on in System Settings > Accessibility > Motion (Display on macOS 15), a style that moves is shown as Quiet, and the line that names the style says so. Quiet, Hairline, Whisper, Hush, Ink, Tint, Solid and Focus do not move and are shown as they are. Settings > Overlay > Pointer styles move with Reduce Motion on lets the other styles move.

Drag a Row Out

Dragging a row out of the overlay gives its link: a trigger’s link shows the trigger in its palette, a palette’s link opens the palette. Holding Option at the start of the drag gives the link that runs it instead. The overlay closes when the drag ends.

  • Automation: Rapidfire works with other tools on the Mac. Other apps can show a palette, add triggers, and read what is kept in Rapidfire, through links, through the Shortcuts app, and by dragging and copying.

Where the Overlay Opens

Settings > Overlay > Opens chooses between Center of the Screen (the screen the pointer is on) and At the Pointer. A palette can have its own choice in Palette Settings.
