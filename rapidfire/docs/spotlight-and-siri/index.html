Spotlight and Siri

Palettes and triggers can be found by name in Spotlight and asked for through Siri, without opening Rapidfire first.

Last updated 20 November 2026

Find a Trigger or a Palette in Spotlight

  1. Press ⌘Space and type the name of a trigger or a palette.
  2. Choose Rapidfire’s item in the results.
  • A trigger’s item opens the trigger. Its second line says which palette it is in.
  • A palette’s item shows that palette’s overlay.
  • The words rapidfire, palette and trigger, and a trigger’s alias, also find these items.

Only names are given to Spotlight, never links, and the index stays on this Mac.

Ask Siri

Say or type “Find” and a name “in Rapidfire”, for example “Find Twitch in Rapidfire”. Rapidfire shows the overlay with what matched. When exactly one thing matches, what happens is set in Settings > General > Siri and Spotlight.

When Find matches a triggerShow Its Palette shows the trigger highlighted in its palette. Run the Trigger opens it.
When Find matches a paletteShow Its Overlay shows the palette. Run the Whole Palette opens every trigger in it.

The General palette is never run as a whole, and a large palette asks first, as set in Settings > Keys > Confirm before opening many.

Keep Something Out of Spotlight and Siri

  • Settings > General > Show palettes and triggers switches all of it off.
  • Palette Settings > Show in Spotlight and Siri does the same for one palette and its triggers.

Actions in the Shortcuts App

Rapidfire’s actions, such as Open Palette, Run Trigger, Add Trigger and Find Triggers, are listed on the Automation page.

  • Automation: Rapidfire works with other tools on the Mac. Other apps can show a palette, add triggers, and read what is kept in Rapidfire, through links, through the Shortcuts app, and by dragging and copying.
  • Settings: Every setting, pane by pane, with what it changes. Settings opens with ⌘, from the overlay, the editor and the menu bar icon’s menu.
