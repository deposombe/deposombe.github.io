Palettes

Palettes group triggers. A palette can have its own hotkeys, a prefix, and a place inside other palettes, which is how a large library stays easy to pick from.

Last updated 22 November 2026

The General Palette

General is always there and cannot be deleted. It is what the menu bar icon and the first hotkey open, and where a dropped link goes unless another palette is chosen. Other palettes can be shown inside it, so General can serve as the one place from which everything is reached.

Make a Palette

Palettes besides General need a license. Without one they are shown with a padlock, and New Palette opens Settings > License.

  • License: Rapidfire works without a license with the General palette. A license, bought once, adds more palettes and everything built on them, on 3 Macs.
  1. Open the editor: choose Open Editor from the menu bar icon’s menu, or press ⌘E in the overlay.
  2. Click New Palette at the bottom of the sidebar, or choose File > New Palette (⇧⌘N).
  3. Type a name and press Return.

Triggers are added to the palette that is selected in the sidebar. To move triggers between palettes, drag them onto a palette in the sidebar, or select them and choose File > Move To.

Quick Add can make a palette as well: type a new name in its palette field and press Return.

Palette Settings

Selecting a palette shows its settings at the side of the editor. View > Show Palette Settings (⌥⌘I) shows or hides them.

IconThe symbol shown beside the palette’s name in the sidebar.
PrefixA short text, such as o or spe, that comes before each of the palette’s names wherever the palette is shown inside another: “o Instagram”. Typing the prefix narrows the list to that palette.
Show InsideThe palettes this one appears in, each as All Triggers or One Entry.
HotkeysGlobal shortcuts that open this palette, each in its own hand mode.
Include in cycleAdds the palette to the round a General hotkey steps through when it is pressed again.
OpensWhere the overlay appears for this palette: Default (Settings), Center of the Screen, or At the Pointer.
Show in Spotlight and SiriWhether the palette and its triggers can be found by name in Spotlight, by Siri and in the Shortcuts app.
From Other ToolsThe link that opens this palette, ready to copy into another app.

Show a Palette Inside Another

Any palette can be shown inside any other, and inside several at once. A palette named Home Assistant can appear in Automations and in General.

  1. Select the palette in the editor.
  2. In Palette Settings, under Show Inside, turn on the palette it should appear in.
  3. Choose Show as: All Triggers lists its triggers there after the prefix; One Entry adds a single row that opens the palette.
  • Prefixes chain. With Home Assistant (prefix h) inside Automations (prefix a) inside General, Lights reads “a h Lights” in General.
  • A palette without a prefix adds nothing to the names; its triggers mix in with the others.
  • A trigger that can be reached in several ways is listed once in each overlay, by its shortest path.
  • A palette cannot be shown inside itself, directly or through others. Rapidfire refuses the change and names the loop.

One Entry keeps a large palette out of the way: the outer list gains one row instead of fifty. All Triggers suits a small palette that should be reachable without an extra step.

Hotkeys

Every palette, General included, can have any number of hotkeys. Each hotkey opens its palette in one hand mode: Both, Left or Right.

  1. Open Settings > Hotkeys, or select the palette in the editor and find Hotkeys in Palette Settings.
  2. In the empty row, choose the hand, click Record Shortcut, and press the shortcut.
  • Pressing a palette’s hotkey again closes the overlay.
  • Pressing the hotkey of another palette while the overlay is open switches to that palette.
  • A shortcut needs Command, Control or Option. A shortcut another app has registered is refused, and the row says so.
  • macOS 15.0 and 15.1 do not accept a shortcut that uses only Option, or only Option and Shift. Add Command or Control, or update to macOS 15.2 or later.

Step Through Palettes with One Hotkey

A General hotkey can step through chosen palettes: General first, then each palette with Include in cycle turned on, in sidebar order, then closed. One hotkey pressed two or three times then reaches the palettes used most, without a hotkey for each.

With no palette included in the cycle, a General hotkey opens General and closes it again.

Order, Renaming and Deleting

  • Drag a palette in the sidebar to change its place, or use File > Move Palette Up (⌥⌘↑) and Move Palette Down (⌥⌘↓). The order decides the cycle and the order in which a whole palette opens.
  • To rename a palette, select it and choose File > Rename Palette.
  • To delete palettes, select them and press Delete. Rapidfire asks first, and Edit > Undo (⌘Z) brings a deleted palette back with its triggers.

Dynamic Palettes

A dynamic palette holds no triggers of its own. It shows another palette and can be switched to show a different one, by hand, at a time of day, or when an app comes forward. It is described on the Automation page.

  • Automation: Rapidfire works with other tools on the Mac. Other apps can show a palette, add triggers, and read what is kept in Rapidfire, through links, through the Shortcuts app, and by dragging and copying.
  • The Overlay: The overlay is the list a hotkey shows. This page describes what is on it, every way of picking from it, and the modes that change how it behaves: Show All, watching, and opening a whole palette.
  • How Keys Work: Rapidfire works out the keys for every trigger from its name. This page explains the rule, how to steer it with aliases, and how to lock the keys of a trigger so they never change.
